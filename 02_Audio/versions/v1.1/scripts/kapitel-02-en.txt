[TEIL I | KAPITEL: Something Missing | ABSCHNITT 1: Under the Ice]

Ear to the wood, the cheek burning with cold.

Inside, the counting goes on, long after the fist has gone still. Four. Five. Six. A thin, high voice that stumbles at seven, as if it had to go and look up what comes next.

"Who's there?"

A whisper against the planks, no more.

The counting breaks off.

Only my own pulse now, knocking against the wood as if it meant to get in alone. Behind the wall a floorboard creaks. Then a silence so complete that the water in the boot turns loud the moment the weight shifts.

"Please."

Nothing comes back.

So I stand there until the cold has wandered out of the wood into my face and the cheek goes numb. Then the body straightens, and the hand finds on its own the place behind the right ear where the skin pulls tight.

A line.

Narrow, smooth, a little raised, like the seam on a piece of leather. The fingertip runs over it, once, twice. At the first waking it wasn't there. That is as certain as the death before it. Swallowing, it tingles underneath, as if something beneath the skin were sitting up to listen.

The hand drops. Returns. The line stays.

Then the ears pop.

Far out this time, and that is new. The pressure begins somewhere in the grey, twenty paces off, more perhaps, and slides closer like water spreading over level ground. The scar tightens before the rest of me understands.

That out there is the thing that let me fall.

Standing still is out of the question this time.

Along the wall, hand on the stone, away from the gate. Slowly at first, then faster. The pressure follows, no stronger, no weaker, always behind me to the left, patient as the cold itself.

The wall bends. For a heartbeat it is lost, then back. Then it ends, or slips away for good, there's no telling which, and ahead there is only mist and underfoot ground that gives.

On anyway. Away from the pressure. Away from the gate.

Ice crunches under the soles. The grass stops. There is only ice now, grey and smooth, with dark patches in it like eyes that never blink. Both boots fill, the toes long since lost. Behind, the crackle in the ears grows quieter, further.

Stop. Listen.

The pressure is almost gone.

A long breath rises, and out of the throat breaks a laugh, short and ugly. It frightens me more than anything before it, and still it won't be held back. It came, and I knew, and I walked away, and it didn't get me.

Then the ice gives.

It only sighs, soft and quiet, and the ground is gone. The water closes over the head before a scream has time. So cold the heart skips a beat and then begins to race. Arms thrash, fingers find ice, but the ice lies above, not beside, and it is smooth, and the fingers slide away.

Black. Above it a grey shimmer that could be the light or could be only the ice.

Hold the air. Count. At fourteen the chest burns. At twenty-one the fingers stop obeying. At thirty the cold becomes a weight that settles over me and presses me down, very gently, like a hand drawing a blanket over a sleeper.

The counting stops.

[TEIL I | KAPITEL: Something Missing | ABSCHNITT 2: The Word]

Frost on the eyelashes.

Under the back, ice, and the ice is whole. The head turns, the crackle is loud, and close beside the face lies a place where the ice is new and thin, black beneath, a circle the size of a man.

Down there is where I was.

The body sits up and waits for the shaking. It doesn't come. I am cold, but the cold lies only on the skin now and goes no further, like rain on a good coat.

The hands come up before the face.

On their backs, white patches, uneven, like rime on a windowpane. Rubbing doesn't help. They are in the skin. When the fingers move, the patches pull a little, and underneath it is warm. Warmer than anywhere else.

A long time I stare at them.

So that's how it is. Die, come back, and bring something with you. Behind the ear a seam that hears things coming. On the hands a frost that keeps the cold out. The body remembers what killed it and arms itself against it.

The ice holds. Carefully back, step by step, counting again so the distance will have a name. At ninety, grass under the soles.

Above, far and milky, the light.

Beneath it, it must stand. The high thing made of stone. The one with the gate.

The mouth opens to say it, just for myself, just to hear it.

Empty.

How it looks is still there. How the wall feels, wet and cold, the joints full of frost. That there are people up top who throw stones. How the wood sounds under the fist.

The word is gone.

Searching for it is like searching for a knife that lay in the hand a moment ago. Feeling along the edges. Finding the place where it lay, still warm, as if someone had only just taken it away. The high thing with the light. Nothing more remains.

Nausea rises. Down into the grass, head between the knees, and the grass is stiff with frost and pricks through the cloth.

I paid. With a word. Whether there are more words left to give, I don't know. How many there ever were, I don't know either.

The thumb runs over the white patches. The warmth beneath.

The high thing has a gate, and the gate is shut. The ones up top throw stones. The thing in the mist lets me fall. And every fall brings something back that makes me harder.

Enough of it, and one day I will stand before that gate, and no one will be able to hurt me anymore.

Then they will have to hear me.

Up. Find the light. Toward it. One. Two. Three.

At seven, someone counts along.

"Seven," somewhere in the grey behind me. "Eight."

I stop.

So does the voice.

Turn around. Mist. No pressure in the ears, no crunch, nothing. Only grey, so thick the outstretched hand blurs inside it.

One step.

"Nine."

Soft. Patient. So close a breath should be audible, if it breathed.
