[TEIL I | KAPITEL: Turned Away | ABSCHNITT 1: The Gate]

The ground comes first.

Hard beneath the shoulder blades, cold down into the spine, and over it a weight of wet cloth that holds me down like a second skin that isn't mine. The air tastes of iron and of water that has stood too long in a hollow. My breath rises and stays above my face, grey, undecided, a small cloud with nowhere to go.

One.

Another breath. Two. Counting works. Counting is the only thing that comes on its own.

At eleven the fingers obey. Under them rime breaks from stalks long dead, a thin and brittle crackle, like glass someone has wrapped in a cloth. The cold climbs through the palm into the bones, joint by joint, until it settles in the wrist and waits there.

At nineteen my back tears itself from the ground. The cloth makes a sound like wet paper, and ice-cold water finds its way into the collar. Teeth knock together until the jaw forces them to stop.

Mist.

It begins where the outstretched hand ends. Beyond it nothing but grey that moves though no wind blows, that breathes without lungs. The world reaches three steps, maybe four. Two knees. Two boots, dark with wet. A patch of brown grass under white rime.

The rest is swallowed.

Somewhere there ought to be a name now. A place, a road that led here, a face. Where all of that should lie there is only a smooth, cold place, and thought keeps returning to it the way a tongue returns to a gap in the teeth.

My hands search without being asked. Chest, belly, the pockets at the hips. Empty. No knife, no bread, no scrap of paper where someone wrote down for me who I am. Only wet cloth, and under it a body that shivers. It is mine because it is all that is left.

Twenty-three. Twenty-four.

To the left, gravel crunches.

The counting breaks off.

A small sound, weight on stones, behind the grey wall. Then silence, and in the silence the ears pop, as if the head were plunging down a mountain the body never climbed. The pressure settles behind the eardrums and stays.

Gravel again. A little further right.

It is circling me.

There is no way to know that. The body knows it anyway. It is already crouched, palms flat on the rime, weight on the balls of the feet, before any choice was made. The pressure in the ears swells and ebbs, slow, in time with a breath that is not mine.

Then it withdraws.

Only when the thighs burn does the breath dare to leave. The cloud hangs in front of me and sinks, heavy, as if it too were afraid to rise.

High above, where the head turns without knowing why, the mist is brighter. A milky smear, hardly more than a guess, like a candle burning behind a curtain, a candle you are not meant to see. It makes the eyes water. The tears go cold on the cheeks.

Up there is something that is not mist.

* * *

The legs carry me, hesitant at first, then out of spite. Toward the light, or toward the place beneath it that can only be guessed in the grey. The mist shifts every direction by a hand's breadth, and with every step the hand's breadth grows. So I count again. Numbers don't shift.

The ground goes soft. At forty-eight the right foot breaks through a crust of ice, and beneath it black water waits, so cold it bites. The bog wants to keep the boot. Only at the third wrench does it give it up, brimming, and from then on it squelches with every step and never warms.

Where grass grows, the ground holds. The body won't say where that knowledge comes from. It knows things no one taught me and keeps them to itself until it needs them.

The light comes no closer. It only climbs higher the further I go, as if someone were drawing it up on a string.

At a hundred and twelve the outstretched hand strikes stone.

No shadow announced it. It is simply there, a wall of rough blocks, black with wet, the joints white with frost. Cold, the stone, colder than the air. The forehead leans against it on its own, and for one breath it is almost good to touch something solid that does not give.

Head back. The wall grows upward until the grey eats it. Somewhere up there, where it ends, the light burns.

A tower.

The word comes out of nowhere, and my throat closes, I am so grateful. A tower. People live in towers. People have fire, and fire is warm.

The hand stays on the stone so the wall won't lose me. Moss under the frost, stiff as leather. Blocks that jut, blocks that retreat. After thirty steps the stone under the fingers turns to wood.

A gate. Planks as thick as a forearm is long, with iron bands across them. The iron is so cold the palm clings to it, and when it tears free, a scrap of skin stays behind. No pain. Not yet.

The fist knocks.

The wood swallows the blow. A dull note wanders inward and loses itself somewhere in the belly of the tower.

Nothing.

Three times, harder. The knuckles meet the wood, and the pain that comes after all is bright and clean, the first thing in all that grey that feels like something.

Forty heartbeats. Then again.

"Hello?"

The voice belongs to a stranger. Rough, as if it had lain in a box for years. How it ought to sound, I can't say. Only not like this.

"Is anyone there? I..."

The sentence has no end. I am. I need. I don't know who.

So I keep knocking, until the skin over the knuckles splits and the wood under the fist turns wet with something that isn't mist. Until the knuckles are white and red beneath. To stop would mean being alone again with the grey and the pressure in the ears and the thing that circles.

[TEIL I | KAPITEL: Turned Away | ABSCHNITT 2: Mistwalker]

High above, stone scrapes on stone.

Then a voice, so distant the mist bends it, yet one word comes down whole, heavy as a blow.

"Mistwalker!"

Nothing to see up there. Only grey, and behind the grey the milky light.

"Please! I don't know where..."

"Mistwalker!" A second voice, higher, then a third. They seize the word and throw it to one another until it sounds like a prayer, like something you say so that a thing will go away and not come back.

No one asks who stands down there. No one asks what he wants.

Something whistles past the ear and slams into the ground, a stone the size of a fist. A splinter leaps against my cheek. Warmth runs down the skin and is cold at once.

One step back. Another.

"I won't hurt you!"

The next stone strikes the gate, and the wood booms like a drum at a burial.

Bleeding hands pressed to my chest, I stare upward. No more words come, only the scraping, the whistling, the dull impacts all around. I have done nothing. Knocked, that's all.

And they knew the name before I had said a word.

Then the ears pop.

Behind me. Sudden, close, a pressure bearing down on the eardrums, on the temples, on the place behind the eyes where thoughts sit. The air turns heavy as wet wool. The body turns, slowly, because it refuses to be quick.

Mist.

Where the pressure comes from, it is thicker. It has a shape that is no shape, like a shadow on black water that nothing casts.

No gravel crunches now. It is too close for sound.

Every muscle wants to run, so badly it twitches. But behind me stands the gate, left and right only stone, and before me stands the thing that stands in the mist.

The hands rise. Maybe to show they are empty.

The ground tilts.

The world leans away beneath me, and the fall begins and does not end. All at once the cold is inside. It fills the chest, the throat, the head, and where air should be there is only cold.

Last of all, the voices on the wall. Quiet now. Almost relieved.

Then nothing.

* * *

The ground comes first.

Iron and standing water. A breath that hangs above the face.

One.

The shaking won't stop, and not only from the cold. The gate is still there, inside me. The stones. The pressure and the tilting and the end of air.

I was dead.

As sure as the chest now rises. And yet here a body lies in the rime and breathes, and above it stands its own grey cloud, going nowhere.

The right boot is full of water, cold as before, the same squelch when the toes move. Before my face, two hands. The knuckles split, the blood on them black and dry. On the cheek a thin crust pulls.

No other place. Here, before the gate, where I fell. The wall beside me in the grey. The wood. The iron bands, where a shred of my skin still clings.

And high above it all, through the mist, the milky light.

A long time I sit like that. Counting breaths and marvelling at each one that comes. Deep down something says this is wrong. That no one comes back. That this body ought to lie where it fell.

But it stands.

Leaving would be easy. Into the grey, anywhere stones don't fall. The mist lies there and looks back, and somewhere inside it waits the thing that let me fall.

I turn to the gate.

They didn't open because they don't know me. Because I am nothing. A word you shout from walls. If I were more than that, strong enough to stand here and not fall, they would have to hear me.

Then they would have to let me in.

The knuckles burn before they touch the wood.

Once. Twice. Three times.

And from inside, through the planks, so faint it might be the rushing in my ears, a voice is counting.

"One. Two. Three."

In time with my blows.
