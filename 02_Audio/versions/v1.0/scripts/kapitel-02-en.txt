[TEIL I | KAPITEL: Something Missing | ABSCHNITT 1: Under the Ice]

I press my ear to the wood.

It is so cold my cheek burns. Inside, the voice keeps counting, even after I have stopped knocking. Four. Five. Six. It is quiet and high, and it stumbles at seven, as if it has to think about what comes next.

"Who's there?" I whisper against the wood.

The voice breaks off.

I wait. I hear only my own pulse beating against the wood, as if it wanted to get in by itself. Somewhere behind the wall a floorboard creaks, then everything is still. So still I can hear the water in my boot when I shift my weight.

"Please," I say.

Nothing more.

I stay like that until the cold has crept out of the wood into my face and my cheek goes numb. Then I straighten. My hand goes on its own to my neck, behind my right ear, where something pulls.

There is a line.

It is narrow and smooth and slightly raised, like the seam on a piece of leather. I run my fingertip over it, once, twice. It wasn't there when I first woke. I know it as surely as I know I died. When I swallow, it tingles there, as if something under the skin were sitting up.

I take my hand away and put it back. The line is still there.

Then my ears pop.

This time it is far off. I hear it coming before it is near, and that is new. A pressure that begins somewhere out in the grey, twenty paces away, maybe more, and slides slowly toward me like water spreading across level ground. The scar behind my ear tightens.

I know what that is. That is the thing that let me fall.

This time I don't stand still.

I walk along the wall, my hand on the stone, away from the gate. Slowly at first, then faster. The pressure follows. It grows no stronger, but no weaker either. It is there, behind me to the left, as patient as the cold.

The wall bends. I lose it for a heartbeat and find it again. Then it ends, or I lose it for good, I don't know which, and ahead of me there is only mist, and under me ground that turns soft.

I keep going anyway. Away from the pressure. Away from the gate.

Ice crunches under my soles. The grass stops. There is only ice now, grey and smooth, with dark patches in it like eyes. Both my boots are full of water. I can't feel my toes anymore. Behind me my ears crackle, quieter now, further away.

I stop. I listen.

The pressure is almost gone.

I breathe out, long, and my cloud rises in front of me, and I laugh. It is a short, ugly sound, and it frightens me more than anything else, but I can't hold it back. I heard it. I heard it, and I walked away, and it didn't get me.

Then the ice gives.

It only sighs, soft and quiet, and the ground isn't there anymore. The water closes over my head before I can scream. It is so cold my heart skips a beat and then begins to race. I thrash. My hands find ice, but the ice is above me, not beside me, and it is smooth, and my fingers slide off.

I see nothing. Only black, and above it a grey shimmer that could be the light or could be only the ice.

I hold my breath. I count. At fourteen my chest burns. At twenty-one my fingers stop obeying. At thirty the cold becomes a weight that settles on me and presses me down, very gently, like a hand.

I stop counting.

[TEIL I | KAPITEL: Something Missing | ABSCHNITT 2: The Word]

Frost on my eyelashes.

I lie on my back. Under me is ice, and the ice is whole. I turn my head, and the crackle is loud, and close beside my face is a place where the ice is new and thin, black beneath, a circle the size of a man.

That's where I was.

I sit up. I wait for my body to shake, and it doesn't. I'm cold, but the cold stays outside. It lies on my skin and goes no further, like rain on a good coat.

I lift my hands.

On the backs of them are white patches, uneven, like rime on a windowpane. I rub at them. They don't come off. They are in the skin. When I move my fingers they pull a little, and underneath it is warm. Warmer than the rest of me.

I stare at them for a long time.

So that's it. I die, and I come back, and I bring something with me. Behind my ear a line that hears things coming. On my hands patches that keep the cold out. My body remembers what killed it.

I stand. The ice holds me. I walk back carefully, step by step, and I count, so I'll know how far I've come. At ninety I feel grass under my soles again.

Then I raise my head and see the light.

It is still there, high up, milky and far. Beneath it, it must be there. The high thing made of stone. The one with the gate.

I open my mouth to say it. Just for myself, just to hear it.

There's nothing.

I know what it looks like. I know how the wall feels, cold and wet, the joints full of frost. I know there are people up top and that they throw stones. I know how the wood sounds when you knock.

But the word is gone.

I search for it the way you search for a knife you were holding a moment ago. I feel along the edges. I find the place where it was, still warm, as if someone had only just taken it. The high thing with the light. That is all I have.

I feel sick. I sit down in the grass and hold my head between my knees, and the grass is stiff with frost and pricks through the cloth.

I paid for it. With a word. I don't know if I have more words to give. I don't know how many I had.

I run my thumb over the white patches on the back of my hand. The warmth underneath.

The high thing has a gate, and the gate is shut. The ones up top throw stones. The thing in the mist lets me fall. And every time I fall, I come back with something that makes me stronger.

When I have enough of it, I will stand before that gate, and no one will be able to hurt me anymore.

Then they will have to hear me.

I stand up. I turn toward the light and walk toward it. I count the steps. One. Two. Three.

At seven I notice it.

Behind me, somewhere in the grey, someone is counting along.

"Seven," says the voice. "Eight."

I stop.

The voice stops too.

I turn around. Mist. No pressure in my ears, no crunching, nothing. Only grey, so thick I can barely see my outstretched hand.

I take a step.

"Nine," says the voice behind me. Soft. Patient. So close I ought to hear it breathe, if it breathed.
