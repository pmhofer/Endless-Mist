[TEIL I | KAPITEL: Turned Away | ABSCHNITT 1: The Gate]

The ground presses against my shoulder blades.

That is the first thing. No thought, no picture. Only that pressure, hard and cold, and the weight of something wet lying on me, pulling me down. I breathe in. The air tastes of iron and of water that has stood too long. I breathe out, and the breath hangs above my face, a grey cloud that will not dissolve.

One.

I breathe in again. Two. I can do that. I can count.

At eleven I move my fingers. They obey slowly, as if they have to be asked first. Beneath them something crackles, thin and brittle, frost on grass that has been dead a long time. I press my hand flat against the ground and feel the cold climb through the skin into the bones, joint by joint, up into the wrist.

At nineteen I sit up.

The cloth on my back tears free of the ground with a sound like wet paper. Water runs into my collar. I am so cold my teeth knock together, and I clamp my jaw until it stops.

Around me is mist.

It is everywhere. It starts where my hand ends when I stretch out my arm, and beyond that there is nothing, only grey that moves without any wind. Three steps, maybe four, and it swallows everything. I see my knees. I see my feet in boots gone dark with wet. I see a patch of ground, brown grass under white rime.

Nothing more.

I wait for something to come to me. Where I am. How I got here. My name.

There is a place where something should be. I can feel it, the way the tongue finds a gap in the teeth, again and again, without wanting to. I reach for it and find only a smooth, cold edge.

I swallow. The sound is loud in my head.

My hands move over my body on their own. Chest, belly, the pockets at my hips. The pockets are empty. No knife, no bread, no slip of paper with a name on it. Only wet cloth, and under it a body that shivers and is mine, because it is the only thing I have.

Twenty-three. Twenty-four.

To the left, something crunches.

I stop counting.

It is a small sound. Gravel shifting under a weight, somewhere behind the grey wall. I turn my head. Mist. I hold my breath, and in the silence my ears pop, as if I had come down a mountain too fast. The pressure sits behind my eardrums and will not let go.

Nothing moves. Then it crunches again, a little further to the right.

It is circling me.

I don't know how I know that. My body knows it before I do. It is already in a crouch, hands on the ground, weight on the balls of my feet, before I have decided to move. I listen. The pressure in my ears swells and fades, like breathing that isn't mine.

Then it is gone.

I stay crouched until my thighs burn. Only then do I dare to breathe out. The cloud stands in front of me and slowly sinks to the ground.

When I raise my head, I see the light.

It is hardly more than a suggestion. Up there, far above me, the mist is brighter in one place, a milky smear, as if someone had lit a candle behind a curtain. I stare up until my eyes water and the tears go cold on my cheeks.

Something is up there. Something that is not mist.

I stand.

[pause]

My legs carry me, unsteady at first. I walk toward the light, or toward where I think the ground beneath it is. It's hard to tell. The mist shifts everything. I set one foot in front of the other and count the steps, because otherwise there is nothing to hold on to.

The ground grows softer. At forty-eight my right foot breaks through a crust of ice, and beneath it is water, black and so cold it bites. I tear the foot out, and the boot fills. With every step after that it squelches, and the water inside it never gets warmer.

I keep walking. I keep to the places where grass grows, because grass means firmer ground. How do I know that? I just know it. My body knows things I don't.

The light does not come closer. It only climbs higher.

And then, at a hundred and twelve, my outstretched hand strikes stone.

I didn't see it coming. It is simply there, a wall of great, roughly hewn blocks, wet and black with damp, the joints white with frost. I lay both hands on it. The stone is colder than the air. I lean my forehead against it and breathe, and for a moment it is almost pleasant to touch something solid that does not give way.

I tip my head back. The wall rises until the mist swallows it. Somewhere up there is the light.

A tower.

The word is suddenly there, and I am so grateful for it that my throat closes. A tower. I know what a tower is. In a tower there are people.

I follow the wall, my hand always on the stone so I won't lose it. After thirty steps the stone under my hand turns to wood.

A gate.

It is wide and tall, made of planks as thick as my forearm is long, with iron bands across them. The iron is so cold that my palm sticks to it when I lay it there. I pull it away, and a scrap of skin stays on the metal. It doesn't hurt. Not yet.

I raise my fist and knock.

The wood swallows the blow. A dull note that dies somewhere inside.

I wait.

Nothing.

I knock again, harder this time. Three times. My knuckles strike the wood, and the pain is bright and clean, the first thing in all this grey that feels like something.

"Hello?"

My voice is a stranger's. Rough, as if I haven't used it in a very long time.

"Is anyone there? I..."

I don't know how the sentence ends. I am. I need. I don't know who I am.

I knock. I knock until my knuckles split and the wood under my fist turns wet with something that isn't mist. I knock until the skin over my knuckles is white and red beneath. I don't stop. If I stop, I am alone again with the grey and the pressure in my ears and the thing that circles.

High above me, something moves.

Stone scraping on stone. Then a voice. It is so far away that the mist bends it, but the word comes through, clear as a blow.

"Mistwalker!"

I tip my head back. I see nothing. Only grey, and behind the grey that distant, milky light.

"I'm..." I shout up, and again the sentence breaks off, because I don't know what I am. "Please! I don't know where..."

"Mistwalker!" A second voice, higher. Then a third. They take up the word and throw it to each other until it sounds like a prayer, like something you say to make a thing go away.

No one asks who I am. No one asks what I want.

Something whistles past my ear and slams into the ground. A stone, the size of a fist. A splinter leaps against my cheek, and I feel something warm run down the skin and turn cold at once.

I step back. Then once more.

"I won't hurt you!"

The next stone hits the gate. The wood booms.

I stand there with my bleeding hands against my chest and stare upward. No more words come down now, only the scraping, the whistling, the dull impacts all around me. I don't understand. I haven't done anything. I only knocked.

They knew the name before I could say a word.

And then my ears pop.

The pressure is back, sudden and strong, behind me. It presses on my eardrums, on my temples, on the place behind my eyes. The air turns heavy. I turn around, slowly, because my body refuses to be fast.

Mist.

Nothing but mist. But the mist is thicker where the pressure comes from. It has a shape that is no shape, like a shadow on water.

I hear no more crunching. It is too close to make sounds.

My body wants to run. It wants it so badly my muscles twitch. But behind me is the gate, and left and right there is only stone, and in front of me is the thing that stands in the mist.

I raise my hands. I don't know why. Maybe to show they're empty.

The ground tilts.

The world leans away beneath me, and I fall, and the fall does not end. All at once the cold is inside. It fills my chest, my throat, my head. I want to breathe and find no air.

The last thing I hear are the voices on the wall. They are quiet now. Almost relieved.

Then nothing.

* * *

The ground presses against my shoulder blades.

I breathe in. Iron and standing water. I breathe out, and the breath hangs above my face.

One.

I lie there and cannot stop shaking, and it isn't only the cold. I remember. The gate. The stones. The pressure in my ears and the tilting of the world and that there was no more air.

I died.

I know it as surely as I know that I am breathing now. And yet here lies my body in the frost, and my chest rises and falls, and above me my own grey cloud stands in the air.

I sit up. My right boot is full of water, cold as before, the same squelch when I move my toes.

Then I lift my hands in front of my face.

The knuckles are split. The blood on them is black and dry, and on my cheek a thin crust pulls where the splinter struck.

I did not wake somewhere else. I woke here, before the gate, at the spot where I fell. The wall stands beside me in the mist. The wood. The iron bands, where a scrap of my skin still clings.

And high above, behind all that grey, the milky light.

I sit like that for a long time. I count my breaths, and with every one I am amazed that it comes. Something deep inside me says this is wrong. That you don't come back. That this body ought to stay where it fell.

But it stands up.

I stand up.

I could go. Away from the wall, into the grey, somewhere stones don't fall. I turn and look into the mist, and the mist looks back, and somewhere in it is the thing that let me fall.

I turn back to the gate.

They didn't let me in because they don't know me. Because I am nothing. A word you shout from walls. If I were more than that, if I were strong enough to stand here and not fall, then they would have to hear me.

Then they would have to let me in.

I raise my fist. My knuckles are burning before they ever touch the wood.

I knock.

Once. Twice. Three times.

And from inside, muffled by the wood, so faint I almost take it for the rushing in my ears, I hear a voice.

It is counting.

"One. Two. Three."

In time with my blows.
